## Pashai: agreement and disagreement coexist

1903 I pp.164-165, PDF indices 461-462; 1920 pp.34-35, indices 47-48. Entry YC1920E-0030.

The earlier note already denies grounds for assuming Polo personally visited the countries and conjectures a Mongol source. Stein, quoted by Cordier, explicitly agrees. Withdrawing a new attribution-update label for this facet was justified.

But the same entry goes on to challenge the necessity of Yule's proposed route through Dorah/Nuksan and Chitral. Stein proposes a more direct route through eastern Kafiristan to Dir with the wording 'may very well'; the earlier note had treated its route as 'almost certain'.

A corroboration-only entry label therefore omits a distinct criticism and qualified alternative. A criticism-only label would erase explicit agreement. The new record separates source attribution, route inference, and alternative reconstruction. It does not establish the actual medieval route.

## Arbre Sec: transmission does not establish adoption

1903 I pp.113 and 128, indices 408 and 425; 1920 p.31, index 44. Entry YC1920E-0024.

The 1903 note identifies an Oriental Plane while already reporting Ernst Meyer's botanical objection and a response. The later entry transmits Houtum-Schindler's 1909 cypress interpretation.

Cordier's following sentence replies about having read Schindler's January 1898 paper, referring to I p.113 of the third edition. That earlier page contains a real bracketed H.C. citation. This verifies a bibliographical reply, not explicit adoption of the cypress proposal.

The supported description is a competing identification transmitted plus an editorial reply concerning prior reading. Do not turn inclusion, citation, acknowledgment, and acceptance into one relation.

## Tun-o-Kain: controversy, not a single replacement

1903 I p.128, index 425; 1920 pp.25-30, indices 38-43. Entry YC1920E-0022.

The later entry reports Sykes changing a view previously adopted from Yule, quotes the 1905 publication, invokes Stewart's opinion dated 1882 and published in 1886, then introduces Hedin as support for Yule. Hedin's competing conclusion is qualified as extremely probable.

The entry preserves competing positions. Editorial inclusion in 1920 is not the date on which each opinion originated or was published. These dates are what the Addenda reports; the underlying articles were not independently inspected in this round. No route is adjudicated as historically correct.

## Great Desert: different evidence licenses different claims

Earlier comparison checked: 1903 I p.202, index 501. Later witness: 1920 pp.48-49, indices 61-62. Entry YC1920E-0049.

Stein interprets Polo's spirit narrative as locally acquired folklore while acknowledging Yule's prior comparative discussion. He separately invokes plane-table/cyclometer measurements, approximately 380 miles, for the distance and number of marches.

The survey-based corroboration of distance must not silently validate the separate inference that Polo heard particular stories locally. Neither establishes that spirits exist. The inspected earlier scope also does not certify the first appearance of the folklore-source attribution across every earlier edition. The 1871 and 1875 witnesses were not opened.

## The 223-entry frame is not a whole-Addenda denominator

An exact search of the frozen text found eight explicit Introduction p./pp. headings outside every one of the 223 frozen entry intervals. They refer to Introduction pp.6, 10n, 19, 19n, 21, 23, 76n and 92. Their headings were visually checked at 1920 printed pp.3, 4, 5, 8 and 11.

These omitted units include substantive travel chronology, Cordier's reply to Sykes, and the identity of Po-lo/Puh-lo in Chinese records. They cannot be dismissed collectively as front-matter noise.

Keep the old 223-entry population for the retrieval experiments actually run on it. It is not a complete population of retrospective interventions in the 1920 book. Do not silently replace it with 231: only one omitted heading class was checked, and remaining omissions, false heads, continuations and Appendix material still require a whole-frame audit. No updated prevalence estimate is authorized.

## Implication for the humanities study

The four readings require proposition-specific targets; separation of cited scholar and transmitting/responding editor; explicit distinction between proposal and adoption; preservation of modality; separation of evidential bases; and multiple time fields.

These are source-grounded historical findings and coding requirements from a purposive panel, not a universal ontology or a proof that unstructured text cannot recover them. Their recoverability remains a separate empirical question. A representation experiment must not manufacture loss by first coding the historical object too coarsely.
