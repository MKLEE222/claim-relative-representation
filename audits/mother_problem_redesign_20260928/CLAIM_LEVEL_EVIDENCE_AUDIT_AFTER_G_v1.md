# Claim-level evidence audit after Module G

Date: 2026-09-28
Status: POST-G EVIDENCE ADEQUACY AUDIT FOR THE MOTHER PROBLEM. NOT A NEW EMPIRICAL RESULT.

## 1. Mother problem under audit

Upper question:

> What computational representations are sufficient for reliable and sustained warranted discovery?

The audit does not shrink this question until the current experiments pass.

The current empirical program has progressively replaced a static representation-only view with a dynamic interface:

    DynamicResearchability(S_t, E_t, A_t, T; K)

where:
- S_t = retained research state;
- E_t = incoming evidence/correction/revision event;
- A_t = lawful source-access operations available at decision time;
- T = declared continuation/discovery task family;
- K = source/version/admissibility contract.

Module G additionally requires an applicability gate:

    Applicable(E_t, S_t; K)

The issue audited here is whether A-G plus the prior R3/Faust evidence are sufficient to support an answer to the mother problem, rather than merely a publishable bounded mechanism claim.

## 3. Mother-problem dimension audit

### 3.1 Representation sufficiency

What is strongly supported:
- current-answer sufficiency and continuation sufficiency are distinct;
- the needed distinction may reside in state, event channel, lawful source access, or their composition;
- identity/binding can matter even when aggregate values are preserved;
- representation structure can contribute target addressability;
- source/version contract constrains event applicability.

What remains missing:
- a prospectively specified positive representation/interface class shown sufficient for a nontrivial family of discovery-and-revision tasks;
- a bounded sufficiency theorem or simulation result tied to that task family, rather than only separation/ablation witnesses.

Disposition:

    NECESSARY-STRUCTURE / SEPARATION EVIDENCE = STRONG
    POSITIVE SUFFICIENCY CLASS = PARTIAL

### 3.2 Reliable

What is supported:
- pinned source hashes;
- exact deterministic evaluators;
- independent implementation checks in several modules;
- matched wrong-binding/locator controls;
- nulls and source mismatches preserved;
- one independent project-level confirmatory transfer.

What is not yet established:
- robust semantic correctness of substantive historical interpretations under competing admissible readings;
- independent historian adjudication or equivalent external semantic validation;
- reliability of an end-to-end discovery policy rather than a deterministic source operation.

Gate-II remains BOUNDED_PARTIAL and must not be upgraded.

Disposition:

    MECHANISM / DOCUMENTARY RELIABILITY = STRONG
    SUBSTANTIVE-INFERENCE RELIABILITY = PARTIAL

### 3.3 Sustained

What is supported:
- delayed source-route dependence across real repository versions;
- natural revision events;
- exact selective updates without collateral changes;
- child-state recovery and transition audit separated;
- source-version applicability exposed.

What remains missing:
- one prospective episode that repeatedly carries research state through multiple heterogeneous events;
- cumulative retention of unresolved alternatives across more than one update;
- horizon/delay stress in which an earlier representation choice is tested several events later;
- evidence that a single declared interface remains sufficient across the sequence rather than each event being solved by a separately tailored arm.

Disposition:

    TEMPORAL / UPDATE PHENOMENA = STRONG
    MULTI-STEP SUSTAINED INQUIRY = PARTIAL

### 3.4 Warranted

What is supported:
- source/provenance binding;
- explicit uncertainty state;
- correction scope;
- parent-source compatibility;
- selective revision;
- historical witness preservation;
- abstention/unresolved states are permitted.

What remains missing:
- a nontrivial substantive conclusion whose warrant depends on multiple evidence relations/defeaters and is prospectively maintained, revised, or left unresolved;
- independent semantic support stronger than the current historical-reading ceiling.

Most A-G tasks concern documentary/editorial state rather than an inferential scholarly conclusion with defeaters.

Disposition:

    DOCUMENTARY / EDITORIAL WARRANT = STRONG
    SUBSTANTIVE INFERENTIAL WARRANT = PARTIAL

### 3.5 Discovery

This is the largest remaining gap.

A/B:
branch grammar is frozen by the evaluator.

B2:
the low-certainty continuation rule is frozen externally.

D-G:
revision/correction events are supplied to the system.

These experiments test whether a system can continue, route, or revise once the relevant task/event has been declared.

They do NOT yet establish that a representation preserves the ability to identify a previously unavailable-to-the-research-state live question from source evidence.

There is source-native branch feasibility in Frankenstein, but autonomous/endogenous question discovery is explicitly NOT TESTED.

Disposition:

    SOURCE-GROUNDED CONTINUATION = STRONG
    DISCOVERY / QUESTION EMERGENCE = INSUFFICIENT

## 5. What the current evidence can already answer

The following bounded answer is now empirically defensible:

> For ongoing source-grounded research, sufficiency is not a property of a static record alone. At decision time, task-relevant distinctions may be carried by retained state, incoming evidence/revision events, lawful source access, and a source/version contract. Warranted selective update additionally requires that the event apply to the declared parent state, that its target and result be determinate, that unaffected evidence remain stable, and that provenance/history required by the task remain recoverable.

This answer is supported across:
- documentary scope continuation;
- uncertainty-sensitive evidence selection;
- version-dependent source executability;
- natural epistemic/topology revision;
- historical print errata;
- an independently selected published digital-corrigenda project.

It is a strong answer to:

    sustained source-grounded researchability and warranted update

It is NOT yet a sufficient empirical answer to the full phrase:

    reliable and sustained warranted discovery.

## 6. What is still required to answer the mother problem strongly

The missing empirical burden is not "more corpora" generically.

Two obligations remain load-bearing.

### H. Prospective independent discovery episode

Use a genuinely fresh scholarly project/corpus after the discovery contract is frozen.

The system receives:
- a root research state/question;
- lawful representation/source access;
- no supplied follow-up question.

The source must contain multiple eligible next distinctions, such as:
- conflicting attributions;
- unresolved relation/status;
- source contradiction;
- uncertainty tied to different evidence;
- revision history that makes one previously unasked distinction live.

Pre-freeze:
- branch eligibility grammar;
- what counts as a new-to-research-state question;
- admissible source routes;
- safe abstention;
- equivalent successful questions;
- stopping conditions;
- evaluation independent of the tested representation.

Primary measures:
- live distinction detected;
- eligible follow-up question formed;
- correct source-bound referent maintained;
- admissible evidence route reached;
- unsupported question/claim rate;
- justified unresolved/stopping outcomes;
- cost/reopenings.

This must not be an LLM-only gold/scorer loop.

### I. Prospective multi-event warranted-discovery trajectory

Preferably on the same fresh project if the source ecology supports it.

A frozen episode should contain at least:
- one discovery-triggering distinction;
- one later evidence/correction event;
- one irrelevant/null event;
- one event that affects only a subset of conclusions;
- at least one unresolved alternative carried across a delay;
- a later task requiring information from an earlier state.

Compare one prospectively specified candidate interface against principled ablations.

Measure:
- current answer;
- discovered branch;
- evidence recovery;
- warrant state;
- selective revision;
- collateral revision;
- unresolved-state persistence;
- transition audit;
- source/version applicability;
- cumulative source/access cost.

The same interface should be used throughout the episode.

This is the direct test of "sustained warranted discovery".

## 7. Positive sufficiency obligation

Separations alone cannot answer "what is sufficient".

Before H/I execution, specify one candidate interface R* prospectively, for example conceptually:

    R* =
      task-addressable target identities
      + source/version provenance
      + relation/evidence bindings
      + uncertainty/status
      + dependency/warrant links required by the declared task family
      + retained unresolved alternatives
      + lawful source handles
      + revision-event preconditions/history where required

This is not a claim that every field is universally necessary.

The sufficiency claim must be relative to:
- declared task family U;
- source/version contract K;
- access operations A;
- horizon H;
- cost/inspection regime B where applicable.

A finite positive claim should have the form:

> For every eligible episode in the prospectively frozen holdout set, R* plus the declared event/access contract determines the required current answer, eligible continuation, warranted update, unresolved alternatives and audit state over horizon H.

Ablations should fail only where their removed distinction is actually required.

If ordinary source reopening makes a leaner R* sufficient, that is a valid result and should reduce the claimed retained-state obligation.

## 8. Adequacy verdict

### For a strong bounded paper on dynamic researchability / warranted selective update

    CURRENT EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE = SUFFICIENT

because:
- multiple development mechanisms exist;
- natural revisions exist;
- nulls are retained;
- state/event/access substitution is causally tested;
- a heterogeneous historical correction pressure test exists;
- one independent project-level confirmatory transfer exists.

### For the full mother problem as stated

    CURRENT EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE = NOT YET SUFFICIENT

The decisive missing pieces are:
1. prospective question/discovery emergence;
2. repeated multi-event sustained inquiry;
3. a positive candidate sufficiency interface over that discovery trajectory;
4. substantive inferential warrant stronger than documentary correction alone.

These are related enough that H and I may be combined into one carefully chosen end-to-end holdout program instead of spawning many more modules.

## 9. Recommended research stop condition

Do NOT continue accumulating unrelated representation-loss examples.

The experimental program should be considered sufficient for a strong bounded answer to the full mother problem when one prospectively frozen, genuinely independent end-to-end program shows:

1. an eligible question not supplied in advance becomes identifiable from the available research state;
2. the system can pursue it through lawful source access;
3. evidence changes a substantive warrant state selectively;
4. unaffected conclusions and unresolved alternatives remain stable;
5. later events can be applied only when source/version preconditions hold;
6. information required after a delay remains available or lawfully recoverable;
7. the same candidate interface R* supports the whole declared horizon;
8. matched ablations expose the predicted failures;
9. null/irrelevant events do not cause gratuitous revision;
10. documentary evaluation is independent of the tested policy/model.

If that program succeeds, additional corpora become robustness/extension work rather than a prerequisite for answering the mother problem in a bounded scientific sense.

If it fails, the failure should revise R* or the sufficiency claim rather than be hidden by adding easier datasets.

## 10. Bottom line

A-G have crossed an important threshold:

    we now have a defensible dynamic architecture
    and an independently confirmed correction mechanism.

They have not yet crossed the final threshold implied by the word:

    discovery.

The next major experiment should therefore be end-to-end and prospective, not another local ablation or another corrigenda corpus.
