# Domain drift and strong-paper gap audit v1

Date: 2026-09-30
Status: AUTHORITATIVE NARRATIVE / SUBMISSION-READINESS AUDIT.

## 1. Audit question

How far is the current project from a strong publishable Digital Humanities paper when compared
with real published work in digital scholarly editing, textual scholarship, provenance and
historical digital resources?

This audit distinguishes:

    scientific maturity
    !=
    manuscript maturity
    !=
    domain identity

## 2. Current scientific object

The strongest current object is no longer the manuscript-v0 object:

    task-relative representation adequacy
    / minimal carrier families

The dynamic line has moved to:

    qualified scholarly action availability

with typed signatures:

    Sigma(g) = (Beta(g), Kappa(g))

and bounded action-relative equivalence:

    fixed admissible event/relation e

    S ~_g S'
    iff
    P_Kappa(g)(S) = P_Kappa(g)(S')

    =>
    Q_g(S,e) = Q_g(S',e)

under the registered models.

Natural sequence evidence further establishes that prior scholarly acts can write the exact
target/state/history conditions read by later acts.

Therefore the paper's mature question is:

> What must a digital scholarly representation preserve about scholarly objects, propositions,
> evidence relations, responsibility and transition history so that later scholars can still
> determine which historically grounded scholarly actions are available, why they are available,
> and how prior acts changed the structure of later inquiry?

## 4. Domain-drift diagnosis

### 4.1 Safe core

Humanities-native core:
- Yule-Cordier editorial history;
- proposition-level criticism, correction, attribution and evidence;
- digital scholarly edition/version/history problem;
- Paper Money natural history-dependent composition;
- Arbre Sec proposition-targeted reply;
- Frankenstein / Whitman / Faust as bounded DH pressure tests;
- questions of editorial provenance, revision and researchability.

These directly instantiate the mother problem.

### 4.2 Useful external validation, not paper identity

Formalization Papers:
- strong independent scholarly-record ecology;
- valuable cross-ecology mechanism confirmation;
- useful proof that the mechanism is not a Yule-specific encoding artifact.

But it should not become the narrative center.

Its paper role should be:

    independent mechanism validation

not:

    defining empirical domain.

### 4.3 High domain-drift risk

TMLR / generic OpenReview / eLife prospective candidates.

Their workflow structures can technically instantiate:
- target binding;
- revision;
- review history;
- state-dependent action qualification.

But treating peer-review infrastructure as the primary empirical answer would shift the paper
toward:

    workflow systems / scholarly communication infrastructure

rather than:

    Digital Humanities / textual scholarship / historical scholarly knowledge.

eLife may be retained as an optional technical prospective holdout if already frozen, but a clean
eLife PASS would not by itself solve humanities-domain anchoring.

Do not use it to replace a humanities-native historical/editorial primary case.

## 5. Published-case comparison

The comparison set is structural rather than citation-count based.

### A. Birnbaum and Spadini (2020)
Reassessing the locus of normalization in machine-assisted collation.
Digital Humanities Quarterly 14(3).

Published-paper pattern:
- starts with a standard textual-scholarship operation: collation;
- identifies an apparently technical step: normalization;
- shows that the technical operation is interpretive and occurs across stages;
- uses the computational pipeline to expose assumptions in scholarly practice;
- contribution remains legible to textual scholars throughout.

Lesson for this project:

    generator / qualification formalism must expose a humanities problem;
    it cannot become the object that readers are asked to care about first.

### B. Bleeker et al. (2022)
Layers of Variation: a Computational Approach to Collating Texts with Revisions.
Digital Humanities Quarterly 16(1).

Published-paper pattern:
- opens from a philological problem: revisions make text nonlinear;
- formalizes that scholarly interpretation;
- builds a computational method;
- tests on a bounded manuscript corpus;
- evaluates whether computational output corresponds to scholarly interpretation.

Lesson:

    one deep humanities-native mechanism + one bounded computational realization
    can carry a strong paper.

This project does not need every experimental asset in the main text.

### C. Broyles (2020)
Digital Editions and Version Numbering.
Digital Humanities Quarterly 14(2).

Published-paper pattern:
- begins from a native scholarly problem: digital editions change after publication;
- asks how their history remains intelligible and citeable;
- relates revision control/versioning to editorial practice;
- uses a digital-edition case and survey to turn infrastructure into an editorial argument.

This is especially close to the current mother problem.

Difference:

    Broyles asks how change should be made visible/citable.

    The current project can go further:
    which retained distinctions preserve the possibility of later scholarly action
    and historical reconstruction after change?

### D. Vancisin et al. (2023)
Provenance visualization: Tracing people, processes, and practices through a data-driven approach
to provenance.
Digital Scholarship in the Humanities 38(3).

Published-paper pattern:
- historical-record transformation is the motivating problem;
- proposes a representation/provenance approach;
- applies it to a historical collection;
- evaluates consequences for interpretation with a user study;
- returns explicitly to DH transparency and interpretation.

Lesson:

    provenance/history must be narrated as scholarly interpretability,
    not as a generic data-state requirement.

### E. Williamson (2026)
Digitized archives, content providers, and slow scholarship: why archival researchers should care
about digital provenance.
Digital Scholarship in the Humanities 41(3).

Published-paper pattern:
- digital mediation is treated as part of sources' textual/history-of-production context;
- the argument is anchored in archival research practice;
- technical histories matter because they change how evidence can be understood and used.

Lesson:

    the paper's broad implication should be future historical inquiry,
    not preservation of abstract transition-state information.

## 7. Where the current project is weaker as a paper

### Gap A — first-page identity

Current manuscript first page still describes:

    task-relative representation adequacy

rather than:

    continuity of scholarly inquiry through representational change.

A strong revised first page must identify:
- the humanities phenomenon;
- the historical/editorial object;
- the failure mode;
- the new conceptual result;
- the evidence strategy.

No generator vocabulary should be required to understand the first paragraph.

### Gap B — one canonical phenomenon

The paper currently has too many valid phenomena.

The main paper needs one canonical humanistic spine.

Recommended:

    Yule-Cordier editorial knowledge history

with:
- Paper Money as the canonical sequence;
- Arbre Sec as the non-resolution/history-only contrast.

Everything else should answer a specific reviewer question.

### Gap C — contribution compression

Current repository contributions can be compressed to three main claims:

C1. Current-state preservation is not sufficient for historical researchability.

C2. The information required for future scholarly action is action-relative:
    binding/state/history requirements differ by scholarly action.

C3. Scholarly actions compose through state/history they create:
    prior acts can change which later acts remain identifiable and lawful.

Do not present the implementation vocabulary as a fourth conceptual contribution.

### Gap D — evidence architecture

Main-text evidence should be tiered:

Tier 1 — humanities-native primary evidence:
    Yule-Cordier / Paper Money / Arbre Sec.

Tier 2 — independent mechanism validation:
    Formalization Papers corrected closed population.

Tier 3 — portability/boundary evidence:
    AAD, VGW, Frankenstein, Whitman, Faust and negative results.

Tier 4 — execution/freshness audit:
    supplement / reproducibility materials.

The current repository often gives Tier 3/4 evidence too much narrative weight.

### Gap E — humanities-neighbor positioning

The related-work/gap section must explicitly distinguish the paper from:

- digital editions as interpretive models;
- edition versioning/revision history;
- provenance disclosure;
- TEI/change tracking;
- scholarly knowledge/provenance ontologies;
- machine-assisted collation normalization;
- general information-quality / view-determinacy theories.

The gap is not:

    history/provenance has been ignored.

The gap is:

    prior work does not give an empirical action-relative account of which distinctions must
    survive for particular later scholarly operations, nor test sequence-dependent future
    researchability through matched losses/repairs.

### Gap F — manuscript evidence discipline

The final paper must not reproduce repository chronology.

It should hide:
- most failed candidate discovery;
- TMLR/OpenReview/eLife screening unless needed in supplement;
- GitHub execution accidents from the scientific narrative;
- module letters;
- engineering repairs;
- every developmental null.

Retain negative results only when they sharpen the conceptual boundary.

## 8. Strong-paper completion distance

The project is scientifically advanced but manuscript-incomplete.

There are four major steps to a strong submission.

### Step 1 — Humanities re-anchoring and claim freeze

Deliver:
- final one-sentence mother question;
- three contribution claims;
- one canonical historical phenomenon;
- explicit role for every secondary ecology;
- domain-drift exclusion rule.

No new empirical result is required.

### Step 2 — Evidence reduction and theorem-to-humanities translation

Deliver:
- one result matrix mapping each main claim to minimum sufficient evidence;
- main-text / supplement split;
- rewrite Sigma(g) / Beta / Kappa into humanities-facing language first, formal notation second;
- retain action-relative equivalence theorem at bounded claim ceiling.

No new corpus is required.

### Step 3 — One integrated manuscript rewrite

Rewrite from scratch rather than patch manuscript_v0.

Required first-order structure:

1. historical/editorial problem;
2. conceptual gap;
3. source and task contracts;
4. Yule-Cordier natural evidence;
5. action-relative representation result;
6. independent ecological validation;
7. implications for digital editions / archival scholarly records;
8. limitations.

This is currently the largest practical gap.

### Step 4 — submission-level red-team

Test:
- first-page DH identity;
- novelty against published neighbors;
- every headline claim has direct evidence;
- no claim depends on post-hoc rescue;
- no unnecessary universal language;
- figures expose humanities mechanism rather than software architecture;
- supplement contains reproducibility/freshness machinery.

Only after this step decide whether an additional clean prospective ecology materially changes the
submission.

## 9. Is another fresh ecology mandatory?

No, not for manuscript construction.

Compared with published DH work, the current project already has more mechanism/evidence than is
normally necessary to write a strong article.

A literal prospective PASS would improve evidentiary cleanliness, especially for a reviewer who
focuses on preregistration.

But it is not the principal reason the current work is not yet a strong paper.

The principal reason is:

    scientific closure has outrun narrative closure.

A non-humanities fresh ecology can even weaken the paper if it expands the domain surface without
answering a new humanities question.
