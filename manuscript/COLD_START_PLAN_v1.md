Date: 2026-09-26

## 1. Paper identity

### Working title

**When Is a Scholarly Representation Enough? Task-Relative Evidence Relations in Digital Humanities**

Alternate title:

**Task-Relative Representation Adequacy: Evidence Carriers, Recovery, and Scholarly Consequence**

Do not put “LLM” in the title.

## 2. One-sentence thesis

> The adequacy of a digital scholarly representation is not a global property of the representation itself, but a task-relative property determined by whether the source-grounded relations needed for a scholarly operation remain available, recoverable, and compositionally usable.

## 3. Paper-level research question

> Which relations must a digital scholarly representation preserve or make recoverable for a declared humanities task to remain evidentially adequate?

Secondary downstream question:

> When representation changes which scholarly arguments are operationally active, can this alter formal claim acceptance under a fixed reasoning contract?

## 4. What this paper is NOT

- not a general theory of historical truth;
- not an evaluation of “best” digital edition;
- not a new provenance ontology;
- not a new argumentation semantics;
- not an LLM benchmark;
- not a claim that richer representations are always better.
