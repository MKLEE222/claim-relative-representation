## 2. Overall editorial judgment

This draft is no longer merely a generic information-systems manuscript with historical examples attached. Its strongest humanistic argument is the contrast between a correction of an earlier critic and a bibliographical reply that must not be converted into assent. Sections 4.1 and 7.3 show what is at stake for an edition: representing the history of discussion without manufacturing a settled identification or erasing an intervention that changes no current answer.

The remaining risk is not the absence of another test population. It is that the prose sometimes defends the formal specification more fully than it explains the editorial interpretation that makes the specification significant. A DH revision should strengthen that interpretation, not replace it with a larger experiment or require an unrestricted action algebra.

The positive publication-level contribution to pursue is: **an inspectable account of which relations must remain recoverable for specified editorial continuations, including continuations that change the record of a discussion without settling its substantive question.** This is an editorial-method contribution with computational realization. It need not be a novel mathematical theorem, a new historical discovery about Polo, or a claim of universal superiority over provenance standards.

Four substantive revisions and two editorial corrections follow. These are priorities within one manuscript revision, not four new research programmes.

## 3. Substantive revision M1 — Say whose resolution the model represents

**Locations:** 2.2, Table 1 explanation, 3.2, 5, and 8.2. Anchors include 'Resolve the represented competition' and the movement from PM01/PM02 to a single current result.

**Existing strength:** The draft already distinguishes Laufer from Cordier and says that resolution is not this article's independent adjudication. That qualification should be retained. The problem is not a total absence of caveats.

**Remaining problem:** Disclaiming historical truth does not fully identify the agent or analytical standpoint of the current state. Laufer's declared correction, Cordier's transmission of it, a local model's adoption of a result for its task, and a community's abandonment of the older view are different things. The inspected transmission warrants the first two; the third is an explicit modeling choice; the fourth is not established by this study.

The source context strengthens rather than destroys the selected reading. In the later Paper Money passage, Laufer distinguishes the acceptability of making paper from Broussonetia from Bretschneider's exclusion of mulberry as a material. His disagreement is proposition-specific, not an erasure of everything the earlier scholar said. The broader passage provides an attributed argument, not merely three polar stance cues. See the existing printed loci, 1903 I:430 and 1920 pp.70–72, and the [Gutenberg reading aid](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/12410/12410-h/12410-h.htm).

**Required revision:** State explicitly that RESOLVE is the registered local transition used to follow the later attributed counter-criticism; it is not evidence of community-wide consensus or of Cordier independently adjudicating every proposition. Identify the particular material claim being normalized and preserve the difference between an attributed assertion and the analyst's current working view. Make this clarification where RESOLVE first enters the prose, not only in the final limitations.

Do not silently change the frozen action enum or claim that an actor-indexed implementation has already been built. This is first a correction of the manuscript's interpretation of the implemented result. If that local interpretation is unacceptable to the author, any stronger implementation claim would need separate evidence rather than a cosmetic rename.

**Completion test:** A reader can answer, without consulting the code, who advances the correction, who transmits it, and what exactly the model has treated as locally resolved. They cannot reasonably read the passage as a claim that the whole scholarly controversy ended.

## 4. Substantive revision M2 — Explain the evidentiary demand, not only the admission rule

**Locations:** 2.2 history ablation; 3.1–3.4; 8.1. The source companion E3 already identifies the relevant unresolved question.

**Existing strength:** The draft correctly discloses the relation-neutral status amendment, the limits of the current-state projection, and the fact that the equivalence implication follows from factorization. It must not be made to claim an unrestricted theorem instead.

**Remaining problem:** The incoming PM03 event already carries a source-interpreted correction relation. A reader can ask why that incoming description does not itself suffice to understand the relation, and why consulting the earlier entry is an additional scholarly requirement rather than merely a rule chosen by the programmer. Repeating 'under the registered contract' accurately bounds the experiment but does not fully answer that interpretive question.

The DH answer is a distinction between **recording what a later source says it is doing** and **substantiating the earlier-source relation against which that action is being reconstructed**. The former can be a legitimate, less demanding task. The experiment chooses the latter. It should explain why that task matters to a historian of an editorial discussion, while acknowledging that another task may permissibly trust or report the later source alone.

**Required revision:** Add one explicit paragraph before the formal definitions. Distinguish the later passage's characterization from retained evidence of the earlier target and its relation. Use the Paper Money source reading to justify the selected comparison; use Arbre Sec to show the cost of conflating a transmitted characterization, a reply, and assent. Keep the possibility of other admissible carriers clear. A chronological log is one realization, not the object of preservation itself.

This is not a demand to prove all scholarly interpretation independent of assumptions. Interpretation-based computational models can be valuable precisely because they articulate and test the consequences of explicit editorial choices. Bleeker et al. explicitly connect their output to the editor's conceptualization and encoding of revision layers; see [their conclusion, paragraphs 88–90](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/16/1/000583/000583.html).

**Completion test:** A reader understands what extra evidentiary question the history-qualified task asks and why the question is worth asking. The manuscript does not infer global irrecoverability from the failure of this registered access route, nor claim that the later source alone contains no information about its target.

## 5. Substantive revision M3 — Engage the closest DH argument earlier and more precisely

**Locations:** introduction, 7.1, and the claim freeze's literature-positioning language.

**Newly localized comparison:** [Broyles 2020, note 43](https://www.digitalhumanities.org/dhq/vol/14/2/000455/000455.html) explicitly observes that the significance of a change depends on a theory of the edited text, the research use, and the interface. This is closer to the present action-relative claim than simply an argument for visible or citable revision history. His main discussion also explains why a corrected reading can remain consequential for scholarship previously based on it.

Consequently, 'different tasks need different information' cannot carry the novelty claim by itself. Nor should provenance scholarship be described as interested only in the past: [Vancisin et al.](https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article/38/3/1322/7140400) explicitly connect transformations to how records can be interrogated and to the research questions users ask. [Williamson](https://doi.org/10.1093/llc/fqag062) treats production and access histories as part of interpreting digitized sources. These are foundations for the present inquiry, not positions it has refuted.

**Required revision:** Move a compact, substantive version of this dialogue into the introduction, after the historical motivation and before the contribution paragraph. Present the extension as an examination of specified next acts: which exact relation and target conditions they require, why two acts can differ, and how a prior intervention supplies conditions for a later one. Do not claim priority over every purpose-relative theory of representation or every provenance model.

Broyles's note 43 is particularly useful because it allows the paper to enter an existing DH problem directly: not inventing use-relative consequences, but rendering selected consequences inspectable at the level of successive editorial interventions.

**Completion test:** The first-page contribution survives a reader who already knows that editions are interpretive and that changes matter differently to different users. The reader can state the additional distinction tested here without saying merely 'history matters' or 'this paper has more experiments'.

## 6. Substantive revision M4 — Let the paired historical readings carry the argument

**Locations:** ordering of 2, 3, 4.1; premature external counts in 4.2; 6 final paragraph; 7.3; repeated limiting sentences throughout the abstract and body.

**Existing strength:** The first paragraph is humanities-facing, and the manuscript does not advertise an algorithmic leaderboard. The mathematical content is already bounded. The problem is not that a DH paper may never contain equations.

**Remaining problem:** The reader receives Paper Money, then an extended qualification/signature/equivalence account, and only afterwards the full Arbre Sec contrast. The historical reason for needing different actions arrives after much of the mechanism that it should motivate. The 120/393/19 paragraph further imports the Formalization ecology before that source has been introduced. Meanwhile, the important argument against manufacturing consensus appears late in 7.3.

**Required local moves:**

1. Bring the historical reading from 4.1 next to the documentary discussion in 2.1, before the extended notation. Retain the later model comparison where it is useful; do not duplicate the whole case.
2. Give the existing historical passages enough context to show the scholarly work of the interventions. Paper Money needs the precise scope of the objection and response, not additional biography. Arbre Sec needs the distinction between the proposal reported from 1909, the reference to the 1898 paper, and Cordier's bibliographical reply; these are not one undifferentiated '1920 update'. Such details are already available in the inspected source range. They should remain attributed through Cordier, without claiming separate inspection of the quoted originals.
3. State what 'later' means here: later relative to a represented documentary stage. Distinguish the historical acts, their later incorporation in an edition, and the order in which a modern analysis reconstructs them. Premature-action tests concern that reconstruction; they are not observations of an alternative real chronology or predictions of unanticipated scholarship.
4. Move the aggregate 120/393/19 coverage paragraph to after the external ecology has been introduced. Preserve the denominators and the existing table data. Keep Sigma and the bounded equivalence statement where they help, but use source-facing names before machine enums.
5. Advance the positive editorial payoff from 7.3: a scholarly representation must distinguish a continuation of discussion from a settlement. Return to it in the conclusion.

**Compression rule:** Keep a short, explicit main-text statement of the corrected reproduction and the absence of independent historical confirmation. Preserve the full INVALID and BOUNDED_PARTIAL records in the supplement. Parser serialization details, repeated all-purpose disclaimers, and long enum names need not consume the argumentative space needed for the historical reading. Moving documentation is not permission to relabel or hide negative outcomes.

**Completion test:** Before meeting the main notation, a textual scholar understands both what can go wrong in the representation and why the two historical continuations should be distinguished. The conclusion answers that problem rather than summarizing test administration.

## 8. Suggested replacement / insertion passages

These are draft prose for author review, not changes already applied to the manuscript. They do not alter the experimental definitions.

### P1 — Positive contribution at the end of the introduction

> The problem is not that digital editions lack histories, but that preserving a history does not by itself specify what a later reader can substantiate from it. We examine that distinction through two kinds of editorial continuation: a correction directed at a previous critic, and a bibliographical reply that does not establish assent. By making their different evidentiary requirements explicit, the analysis shows how a representation can preserve an intervention without either losing its target or turning the continuation of discussion into its settlement.

Placement: after a compact, accurate engagement with the neighboring DH literature. This sharpens the existing C1–C3 structure; it is not a fourth contribution.

### P2 — Authority of the local Paper Money transition

> The sequence records attributed interventions, not successive community-wide beliefs. Laufer advances the later correction, and Cordier transmits it. RESOLVE names the consequence assigned to that intervention within our declared local model of the material-identification dispute. It does not establish that all participants accepted Laufer's account or that the earlier criticism ceased to matter as a historical object. The model's current result must therefore be read together with the attribution and intervening record that make it intelligible.

Placement: before or immediately after Table 1. Retain the existing statement that PM03 is a compound event. Check that any final wording about retained attribution accurately matches the registered source and history outputs; do not imply an additional actor-indexed state implementation.

### P3 — Why the earlier evidence matters despite the later relation label

> A later source's description of its own intervention is not identical to a reconstruction of the earlier record that it addresses. One may legitimately record that the source presents itself as correcting a prior criticism. The more demanding task examined here asks whether the retained earlier-source evidence substantiates the target and critical relation presupposed by that description. Removing this evidence blocks that reconstruction under the registered access conditions; it does not make the later passage meaningless or establish that no other carrier could support the same inquiry.

Placement: between the historical comparison and the formal definitions. This supplies a positive rationale for the contract rather than another generic disclaimer.

### P4 — Future relative to a represented stage

> Here a later action is later relative to the documentary stage being reconstructed. The analysis does not predict what scholars will ask next. It examines which relations must remain recoverable for a specified continuation to be represented with its historical grounds, and how an earlier intervention supplies some of those grounds.

Placement: at the beginning of the methods discussion or Section 5, avoiding repetition elsewhere.
