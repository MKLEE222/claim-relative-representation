# Humanistic significance registry v1

Date: 2026-09-23

This registry asks whether the frozen claim families matter independently of the representation experiment. It does not use intervention outcomes.

## Paper money

The paper-money case is independently consequential in both Polo scholarship and Yuan economic history.

Hans Ulrich Vogel's *Marco Polo Was in China: New Evidence from Currencies, Salts and Revenues* devotes a full chapter to paper money in Yuan China and an appendix to variant manuscript/print passages on the production and use of paper money in Polo's account. That makes the evidential status of Polo's monetary description part of an established historical-authenticity problem, not an experiment-created claim.

Recent economic history also continues to use Yuan paper money as a substantive object of study. Guan, Palma, and Wu (2024) reconstruct the Yuan monetary regime and explicitly cite Vogel's treatment of Marco Polo in their source discussion.

Sources:
- Hans Ulrich Vogel, *Marco Polo Was in China* (Brill, 2013): https://brill.com/display/title/21215
- Appendix on Polo paper-money passages: https://brill.com/display/book/9789004236981/B9789004236981_010.xml
- Hanhui Guan, Nuno Palma, Meng Wu, “The rise and fall of paper money in Yuan China, 1260–1368,” *Economic History Review* (2024): https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ehr.13305

**Gate H disposition:** PASS for the paper-money claim family.

## Kinsay / Hangzhou eyewitness status

Whether Polo actually travelled in China, and what textual details can count as evidence of first-hand presence, is an established scholarly dispute.

Igor de Rachewiltz's critical appraisal of Frances Wood's *Did Marco Polo Go to China?* explicitly frames the authenticity question as a substantive Polo-studies controversy. Vogel's 2013 study also devotes a chapter to Hangzhou tax revenue as evidence in the China-authenticity problem.

The Kinsay claims in this project are therefore not manufactured merely because they are convenient for an attribution intervention. They bear on an existing question: when can the Kinsay material be used as eyewitness rather than transmitted/reportorial evidence?

Sources:
- Igor de Rachewiltz, “F. Wood's *Did Marco Polo Go To China?* A Critical Appraisal” (2002): https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/e70f6bcf-b65a-457e-a871-56eacb668e19
- Hans Ulrich Vogel, *Marco Polo Was in China* (2013), including the chapter “Tax Revenue of Hangzhou and Its Territory”: https://brill.com/display/title/21215

**Gate H disposition:** PASS for the Kinsay eyewitness claim family.

## Coal / Richthofen editorial accretion

The coal case is independently consequential because the named nineteenth-century source, Ferdinand von Richthofen, occupies a documented place in the history of coal, imperial science, and geopolitical interpretations of China.

Shellen Xiao Wu's work shows that Richthofen's Chinese coal surveys helped recast China as a site of enormous mineral potential and links his geological work to the late-nineteenth-century relationship among science, industrialization, and empire. This makes it historically meaningful to distinguish Polo's medieval fuel observation from the later editorial projection attached to Richthofen's industrial-geopolitical horizon.

Sources:
- Shellen Xiao Wu, “The Search for Coal in the Age of Empires: Ferdinand von Richthofen's Odyssey in China, 1860–1920,” *American Historical Review* 119.2 (2014): https://academic.oup.com/ahr/article-abstract/119/2/339/44111
- Shellen Xiao Wu, *Empires of Coal: Fueling China's Entry into the Modern World Order, 1860–1920* (Stanford UP, 2015): https://www.sup.org/books/history/empires-coal
- Chapter “Ferdinand von Richthofen and the Geology of Empire”: https://academic.oup.com/stanford-scholarship-online/book/19297/chapter-abstract/177828109

**Gate H disposition:** PASS for the coal accretion claim family.

## Editorial revision as a historical object

The Yule-Cordier edition history itself has historical warrant as an object of study. Paul Pelliot's 1904 review explicitly describes the third edition as a scholarly revision intended to incorporate the scientific work of the preceding twenty-five years, remove material made obsolete by later research, and introduce newer solutions. This independently supports treating editorial update relations as historically substantive rather than as modern computational annotations.

Source:
- Paul Pelliot, review of Yule/Cordier third edition, *Bulletin de l'École française d'Extrême-Orient* 4 (1904), 768–772: https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_1904_num_4_1_1391
