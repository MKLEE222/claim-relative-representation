# Nearest-neighbor audit: researchability and scholarly workflows

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: literature-positioning amendment.

## Researchability is not a new term

The GLOBALISE infrastructure explicitly frames one of its missions as moving beyond searchability to researchability by combining digital methods with historical contextualisation, structured reference data, annotations, thesauri, and ontology-backed relations.

Humanities workflow literature likewise treats discovery, chaining, browsing, comparing, referring, assessing, and contextual movement through sources as core scholarly activities.

Therefore this project must not claim novelty for:
- the word researchability;
- the idea that contextualisation is needed beyond keyword search;
- the idea that digital infrastructure should support scholarly activities rather than retrieval alone.

## Sharper candidate gap

The project's possible contribution is an auditable decomposition of researchability for a specific historical task.

Given:
- a representation R;
- a scholarly task tau;
- a recovery/source contract K;
- a realistic research entry state E0;

the project asks:
- which source-bound relation q is required;
- which lawful carrier bases preserve q;
- whether one basis can substitute for another;
- the minimum documentary boundary required for recovery;
- whether the historical answer remains the same while the recovery profile changes;
- whether the relation becomes ambiguous/non-determined;
- whether a limited source-bound repair restores the task and rejects an equally shaped incorrect repair.

## Relationship to GLOBALISE

GLOBALISE is an infrastructural programme for increasing researchability through historical contextualisation at scale.

This paper should be complementary rather than competitive:
- GLOBALISE asks how richer contextual infrastructure enables historical research;
- this project asks how to audit whether a particular representation preserves the source-bound relations required by a particular research path, including when textual search remains possible.

## Relationship to scholarly workflow studies

Existing work identifies recurring activities such as discovery, chaining, browsing, comparing and referring.

R3 can use those activities to justify its entry states and mediation routes, while adding a source-bound preservation question:

Does a representation preserve the ability to perform that scholarly activity on this historical object, from this starting state, without evaluator-only knowledge?

## Manuscript consequence

Use 'researchability' as inherited vocabulary, not a branded invention.

Novelty, if established, lies in recovery certificates, separation witnesses, carrier substitution, profile non-equivalence, and constructive repair tied to editorial knowledge history.
