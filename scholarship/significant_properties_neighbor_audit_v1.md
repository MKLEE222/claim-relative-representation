# Nearest-neighbor audit: significant properties and preservation intent

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: literature-positioning amendment; not a novelty result.

Digital-preservation research on significant properties is a direct intellectual neighbor. Its core problem is which characteristics of a digital or intellectual object must survive over time so that it remains accessible, usable, meaningful, and acceptable as evidence.

Relevant traditions include significant properties / essential characteristics, the National Archives of Australia Performance Model, InSPECT, PLANETS/PLATO preservation planning, OAIS Representation Information and Designated Community, and PREMIS-oriented preservation metadata.

## Consequence for novelty

The project must not claim novelty for asking what to preserve, for preservation relative to user/function requirements, or for evaluating transformations against preservation requirements.

## Candidate distinction

This paper can contribute a narrower humanities-specific capability if it:
- defines a source-facing historical task and realistic research entry state;
- treats source-bound documentary relations rather than generic object properties as the recoverability target;
- exposes alternative lawful carriers;
- executes recovery under a declared contract;
- distinguishes answer equivalence from recovery-profile equivalence;
- provides collision/separation witnesses for non-determinacy;
- constructs source-bound repairs with matched wrong-binding controls;
- connects these results to substantive editorial knowledge history.

## R3 fit

The significant objects in Yule-Cordier include responsibility relations: who speaks, what earlier claim is targeted, which authority is cited, which editorial layer introduced the intervention, and whether a later act criticizes, corrects, qualifies, corroborates, or merely adds evidence.

Candidate positioning sentence:

Digital-preservation research asks which properties must survive a transformation for an object to remain meaningful and usable to a designated community. We ask a narrower downstream question: for a concrete historical research operation, which source-bound relations must remain explicit or reconstructible from a realistic research entry state, through which lawful carriers, and with what repair when those relations cease to be recoverable?
