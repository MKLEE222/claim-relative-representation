# Research Contract v0.1

## Research object

The project tests a **Claim-Evidence Dependency Layer (CEDL)** between a digital representation and a humanistic claim state.

For a frozen humanistic claim (C), CEDL records the source-grounded relations that must remain explicit, recoverable, unresolved, or unavailable for that claim to be adjudicated.

The project does **not** assume that richer representations are globally better. Adequacy is claim-relative.

## Frozen acceptance gates

### H — Humanistic significance
A claim must be independently meaningful to the historical/literary problem before representation outcomes are inspected.

### T — Transformation realism
A representation intervention must correspond to a defensible digital operation or documented object transformation. Deliberate sabotage is not admissible.

### V — Claim-evidence validity
Every load-bearing claim-evidence relation requires a source witness. A relation lacking a verified source basis is BLOCKED, not inferred from downstream results.

### S — Selective causality
For an intervention affecting relation set Δ, a target claim must depend on Δ while a matched negative-control claim must not. The control should remain stable.

### R — Restoration
If an intervention changes a claim state, restoring the implicated relation(s) should be tested. Exact, partial, failed, and blocked restoration are retained as distinct outcomes.

## Freeze discipline

1. Claims and dependency sets are committed before intervention outcomes.
2. Source adjudication is stored separately from automated validation.
3. BLOCKED and failed cases remain in the ledger.
4. A failed gate is never repaired by changing the scientific target after seeing the result.
5. Machine-readable validators check research-contract consistency; they do not adjudicate historical truth.
