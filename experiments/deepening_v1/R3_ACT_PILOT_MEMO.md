# R3 act-level pilot memo

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: source-grounded pilot; not the complete R3-A distribution.

## Finding

The first close inspection already shows that the 1920 Addenda cannot be responsibly summarized with one relation label per entry.

Three examples:

### Sarai
One Prologue entry carries two separate objections to the Yule/Cordier identification:
- Friedmann objects on route plausibility;
- Pelliot objects on linguistic/etymological grounds.

The humanistic object is the same identification, but the evidential bases and responsible scholars differ.

### Paper money
The entry contains at least two analytically distinct Laufer acts:
- defeat Bretschneider's objection;
- positively corroborate Polo with additional evidence.

"Correction" and "corroboration" are therefore not interchangeable labels.

### Kinsay
The same p. 193 entry contains:
- Moule's re-identification of an atlas toponym;
- Tanner's argument that Polo may not have personally visited Hang-chou and instead derived the account from another source.

These affect completely different historical questions: cartographic identification versus eyewitness/source authority.

## Consequence

R3-H3 will report both:
- entry prevalence: how many editorial entries contain each intervention family;
- act prevalence: how many explicit intervention acts of each family occur.

Act prevalence is descriptive and clustered by entry.

R3-H1/H2 must operate on act/target/actor edges, not entry-level topic labels.

This strengthens the core representation problem:
preserving the text of an entry is insufficient for responsibility history if actor, target, and relation attachment are not recoverable.
