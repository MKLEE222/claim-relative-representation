## Main finding

The result establishes a workflow-level distinction that entry-level evaluation alone does not expose:

[
entry discoverability 
eq proposition	ext{-}carrier completeness.
]

Under the later-layer condition the correct historical entry is the first ranked result, yet the second evidence carrier required to answer the full frozen inquiry does not appear until rank 35.

Thus:

[
top entry hit 
otRightarrow complete evidence exposure.
]

This is not information-theoretic loss. The underlying Gutenberg entry contains both carriers and direct close reading recovers the historical distinction.

The observed mechanism is **partial exposure under fixed chunking and ranking**:
a 180-word workflow representation can surface one proposition/evidence component while another component from the same source entry remains outside a smaller inspection budget.

## Relation to the controlled evidence-binding separation

Controlled R3 showed that removing evidence-to-proposition bindings can make the Great Desert inquiry underdetermined.

AW1 does not naturally delete those bindings from the source.

This component audit adds a distinct ecological mechanism:

- source object: both carriers survive;
- target entry: highly discoverable;
- top-ranked chunk(s): only a subset of the inquiry's proposition-level evidence may be exposed;
- broader inspection / source-entry expansion: the remaining carrier is recoverable.

The result therefore supports a multi-stage account of researchability rather than a generic loss claim.

## Claim ceiling

Supported:

> A workflow can successfully retrieve the correct scholarly entry while failing, at the same inspection budget, to expose all proposition-specific evidence needed for the registered inquiry.

Not supported:
- the researcher must answer incorrectly;
- the measurement carrier is absent from the representation;
- all fixed-window retrieval has this property;
- rank 35 is a universal practical threshold;
- the controlled evidence-binding deletion is naturally reproduced.

## Scientific consequence

For researchability audits, retrieval evaluation cannot stop at target-entry Hit@K when the scholarly task requires multiple proposition-specific carriers.

At minimum, distinguish:

1. entry discovery;
2. carrier-component exposure;
3. carrier composition under an inspection budget;
4. relation adjudication after the evidence is supplied.
