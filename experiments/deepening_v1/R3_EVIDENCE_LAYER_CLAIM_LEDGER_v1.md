# Combined bounded answer

The strongest currently licensed synthesis is:

> The researchability of an editorial knowledge history is not exhausted by whether relevant text survives a transformation. It depends on proposition-level determinacy, on whether source-grounded carriers remain available or reconstructible, and on whether those carriers can be lawfully composed with a scholar's historical entry state. In the Yule-Cordier case, controlled removal of proposition bindings can make historical inquiries underdetermined; the natural Gutenberg plain-text object preserves alternative carriers for those same distinctions; a frozen access workflow nevertheless produces strongly case-dependent discoverability; and a source-native page pointer selectively repairs one lexical-access failure.

This is stronger and more precise than "more metadata is better" or "plain text loses context."

It is also narrower than a universal theory of digital representation.
