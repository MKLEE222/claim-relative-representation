# Exploratory Claim Map v1

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: SCIENTIFIC EXPLORATION ONLY. This is not a manuscript outline and does not freeze final claims.

## Mother question

Under transformations of scholarly digital representation, which source-bound relations must remain explicit or reconstructible, through which lawful research routes, for a later scholar to discover and reconstruct how claims were attributed, contested, qualified, corrected, corroborated, and maintained from a realistic research entry state?

## Candidate central claim

Representational adequacy for scholarly research is not a property of the digital object alone. It is indexed by a historical task, a research entry state, and a recovery contract. A transformation may preserve textual content and even the final answer while changing the lawful routes by which source-bound scholarly relations can be discovered, reconstructed, attributed, or composed.

Status: STRONG CANDIDATE, NOT FROZEN.

Current support:
- fixed-endpoint versus discovery-state failures in paper-money/Kinsay;
- R3 native-pointer versus pointer-hidden lexical recovery;
- prospective substitution/complementarity results across three duplicate-pointer groups;
- Whitman endpoint-versus-certainty separation;
- Faust relation-presence versus source/composability separation.

Still required:
- corrected R3 historical frame;
- proposition-level genealogy on verified witnesses;
- at least one R3 case where responsibility/modality/evidence-target recovery, not merely target localization, is tested;
- clear prior-art boundary against researchability/significant-properties/workflow literature.

## Claim family H — historical/editorial knowledge

### H1 — heterogeneous editorial maintenance

The 1920 Addenda is not adequately described as a uniformly corrective layer. Already inspected entries contain corroboration, criticism, qualified alternatives, new evidence, bibliographical replies, errata, transmitted competing identifications, and nested expert contributions.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED by purposive inspected cases. No prevalence claim.

### H2 — proposition-specific direction of change

One editorial entry or one later scholar can agree with an earlier proposition while contesting another. The direction of change must therefore be indexed by proposition and target, not by whole entry, whole scholar, or whole edition.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED. Pashai and Temple provide direct examples.

### H3 — transmission is not adoption

Editorial inclusion, citation, transmission, acknowledgment, reply, and adoption are distinct historical acts. A cited scholar's proposition must not automatically become the transmitting editor's commitment.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED. Arbre Sec and Temple are current anchors.

### H4 — evidential basis is proposition-specific

Evidence supporting one proposition in an entry does not automatically warrant another proposition in the same entry.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED. Great Desert is the current clearest anchor.

### H5 — current 223-entry frame is not a whole-book denominator

The frozen 223-entry frame is a reproducible search universe for completed experiments but is not a validated complete population of 1920 retrospective interventions.

Status: SUPPORTED NEGATIVE. Omitted headings, merged units and cross-section contamination are directly verified.

## Claim family M — researchability and representation

### M1 — entry-state relativity

A representation may be adequate when answer-bearing passages are already supplied yet inadequate or materially different when the scholar must discover the later evidence from a realistic historical starting point.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED / GENERALIZATION OPEN.

### M2 — carrier systems, not single important fields

Source-grounded scholarly relations can be supported by multiple lawful carriers. These carriers may substitute for one another or may be complementary.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED in R3; external generalization remains open.

### M3 — output equivalence does not imply recovery-profile equivalence

Two representations or routes can recover the same target while differing in candidate exposure, documentary boundary, assumptions, responsibility traceability, modality fidelity, or traversal structure.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED. Gulf-diet and related R3 cases are direct operational examples.

### M4 — research-support capabilities are separable

Scope inclusion, discoverability, relation determinacy, responsibility/provenance traceability, resolution-state fidelity, and composability should not be collapsed into one scalar adequacy score.

Status: SYNTHESIS CLAIM with executed cross-case anchors; requires careful positioning as an empirical decomposition, not a universal ontology.

### M5 — source uncertainty differs from analyst uncertainty

Historical uncertainty, multivoice contestation, and analyst/coder unresolved status are different states and must be represented separately.

Status: MEASUREMENT CLAIM; source-grounded and aligned with established DH uncertainty practice, not a novelty claim.

## Claim family R — repair

### R1 — source-bound repair can be constructive and falsifiable

When a required relation becomes non-recoverable under a declared contract, a source-bound bridge can sometimes restore the task; an equally shaped wrong bridge should fail and an unaffected task should remain stable.

Status: SUPPORTED_BOUNDED in Whitman/Faust R1. Not yet closed in R3 historical core.

## Claim family X — transfer

### X1 — the audit vocabulary is not unique to Yule-Cordier

Independent DH infrastructures expose related distinctions among native carrier structure, reduced projections, source locators, certainty states, grouping, and composability.

Status: SUPPORTED AS TRANSFER OF AUDIT QUESTIONS, not as one pooled effect size or universal mechanism.

## Claims to demote or retire

### D1 — 'Adequacy is claim-relative' as headline novelty

Keep as background. It is too weak and too close to established task-/function-relative preservation and query-determinacy ideas to carry the paper.

### D2 — CEDL as the central scientific object

The original CEDL remains useful as a ledger, but its claim-level dependency coordinates are too coarse for the current historical evidence unless extended to proposition targets, actor/mediator distinction, evidence basis, modality, entry state, and carrier systems.

### D3 — one global minimal carrier

Retire. Only local minimal sufficient carrier families under a fixed task, contract, decoder and entry state are admissible.

### D4 — 'unstructured text loses scholarly relations'

Unsupported as a general claim. Current R3 results show substantial textual substitution and complementarity.

### D5 — '1920 reverses 1903' as a general editorial story

Retire. Reversal is case-specific; maintenance includes continuity, corroboration, contestation, qualification and nested responsibility.

### D6 — 223 as the historical denominator

Retire for whole-book humanities claims. Preserve only as the frozen universe of already-run retrieval experiments.

### D7 — LLM selective warrant restoration

Not supported by the frozen 45-call experiment. Keep the null.

### D8 — formal argumentation as empirical rescue

Not admissible. Formal attack/reinstatement remains a conditional illustration whose result depends on the mapping.

## Current strongest three-part scientific architecture

1. HUMANISTIC: scholarly editions maintain histories of responsibility, disagreement, qualification and evidence at proposition level; those histories are part of the research object, not mere metadata.

2. REPRESENTATIONAL: preserving text or a final answer does not necessarily preserve the conditions and lawful routes by which that history can be researched from a realistic starting state.

3. MECHANISTIC: those research routes are carried by source-grounded carrier systems that can exhibit substitution, complementarity, ambiguity, profile change, and sometimes repair.

These three claims are mutually constraining. None should be finalized before the corrected R3 frame and proposition-level historical inventory are stable.
