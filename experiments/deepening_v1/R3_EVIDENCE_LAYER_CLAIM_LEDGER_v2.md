# Cross-layer scientific answer

The strongest currently licensed mother-problem answer is:

> What must survive representational change is not a fixed maximal set of metadata fields. For a declared scholarly task and research entry state, there must remain a sufficient, source-grounded carrier system from which the task-relevant epistemic relations and statuses can be determined or lawfully recovered, and a workflow capable of reaching those carriers. Different transformations may preserve content while changing relation determinacy, certainty/status, discoverability, or downstream executability; alternative carriers can sometimes restore the required capability.

Formally, researchability should be indexed by at least:

[
Researchability(R,	au,S,K,pi)
]

where:

- (R): representation;
- (	au): scholarly task;
- (S): research entry state;
- (K): admissible carrier/evidence contract;
- (pi): access/transformation workflow.

There is no licensed scalar claim that one representation is globally "better."

# Stop rule after the natural Gutenberg null

The project must **not** continue searching specifically for a positive natural Yule-Cordier proposition-binding deletion merely to make E1 and E2 look more symmetric.

After E2 established that the real Gutenberg object preserves alternative carriers, outcome-driven search for a natural deletion analogue risks confirmation bias.

A future independently motivated natural binding-loss case may strengthen generalization, but it is not required for the bounded mother-problem mechanism claim.

E5 now supplies an independent ecological demonstration that relation presence, epistemic/status state, and executable representation can naturally separate in a real scholarly infrastructure.
