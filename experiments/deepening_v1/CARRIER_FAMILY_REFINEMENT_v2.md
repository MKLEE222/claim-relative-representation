# Carrier-family refinement v2 — substitution and complementarity

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: conceptual refinement grounded in executed R3 results; not a novelty theorem.

## Problem

Earlier work often spoke of a minimal carrier as if the task had one required coordinate or one interchangeable source route.

R3 now shows two different structures.

### Substitution

Alternative bases independently recover the task output.

Example pattern:
`{pointer}` OR `{lexical carrier}`.

R3-F/G entry-state v1 shows this in several targets: hiding the pointer still leaves a lawful textual route under the registered budget.

### Complementarity

No individual coarse carrier produces the registered exact output, but a composed set does.

Prospective R3 composition v1:
- pointer alone -> two candidates;
- global lexical route -> ranks 11 or 19;
- pointer + lexical discrimination -> rank 1 within candidate set.

## Minimal sufficient carrier family

For a fixed task, contract and entry state, retain the existing set-family view:

`M(tau,K,E0) = {S : S is sufficient and no strict subset is sufficient}`.

The refinement is interpretive:
- multiple singleton sets imply substitution/redundancy;
- a minimal set with more than one carrier implies complementarity under the declared decoder/task;
- several multi-carrier minimal sets imply both substitution and complementarity.

Do not collapse the family to one 'important field'.

## Why entry state matters

A set may be sufficient under RELATION_KNOWN but not under HISTORICAL_START because the target must first be discovered.

Therefore minimal carrier families are indexed by entry state.

## Humanistic interpretation

Editorial infrastructure can support research through composed scholarly operations:
- follow a page/note pointer;
- use wording/referent identity to distinguish among nearby candidates;
- inspect responsibility/stance markers;
- traverse edition sequence.

The task-relevant object is the research route, not maximal metadata retention.

## Claim ceiling

This formalization organizes the observed mechanisms. It is not claimed as a new general theorem in information theory, database determinacy, or preservation science.
