## 1. Priority decision

R3 is the primary research programme.

R1 and R2 are not co-equal narrative pillars. They serve as methodological validation and adversarial pressure tests for concepts first motivated and defined by R3.

The manuscript should therefore be organized as:

1. historical/editorial knowledge problem;
2. task definitions derived from that problem;
3. representation/recovery method;
4. R3 empirical historical findings;
5. R1/R2 external infrastructure validation;
6. computational stress tests and formal boundaries.

## 2. Central humanistic object

The object is the editorial history of knowledge in the Yule–Cordier Marco Polo tradition.

The study asks how claims are:
- introduced;
- attributed;
- criticized;
- corrected;
- qualified;
- reidentified;
- textually revised;
- and incorporated into later scholarly editions.

The key question is not whether a string can be retrieved, but whether a later researcher can reconstruct who made which claim, which earlier locus it addresses, what evidence was invoked, and how the scholarly responsibility changed over time.

## 4. R3-A — complete editorial intervention inventory

For every candidate record:
- verify the backward locus/pointer;
- identify the earlier textual/editorial object addressed;
- record the later actor and cited authority where explicit;
- classify relation family only from source wording;
- record unresolved and multi-label cases;
- retain additive/noncontroversial entries.

Primary output:
distribution of intervention types across the 1920 retrospective layer.

This is a humanistic result independent of any representation experiment.

## 5. R3-B — editorial genealogy

Select episodes by frozen relation-family criteria after R3-A classification, not by desired computational outcome.

For each selected episode, trace the claim across available Yule/Cordier stages:
- 1871;
- 1875;
- 1903;
- 1920.

Record:
- wording change;
- note addition/removal;
- source authority;
- attribution;
- certainty/modality;
- identification;
- explicit dispute/correction;
- unresolved status.

The output is a genealogy of a scholarly claim, not a binary truth label.

Minimum close-reading set:
- one correction/reinstatement episode;
- one attribution/eyewitness episode;
- one identification update;
- one additive/accretion episode;
- one unresolved/nonreversal episode.

Paper money may fill one slot only.

## 6. R3-C — representation consequences on humanistic tasks

Humanistic tasks are frozen from R3-A/B:

H1 RESPONSIBILITY:
recover earlier locus + earlier actor/source + later actor/source + relation.

H2 TRAJECTORY:
recover ordered scholarly acts across layers without collapsing base narrative, editor, cited scholar and later response.

H3 CHANGE CHARACTERIZATION:
recover the intervention-family distribution for the complete verified candidate frame.

Representations:
- FULL_LAYERED;
- LINEAR_TEXT;
- TOPIC_BAG.

A representation failure is load-bearing only when:
- a natural collision exists under the complete visible input; or
- a controlled twin preserves visible text while changing the required source-bound relation.

No weak-retriever failure is treated as information loss.

## 7. R3-D — source-bound repair

For each demonstrated representation separation:
- restore only the missing source-bound attachment/attribution relation;
- verify exact H1/H2 recovery;
- compare with an equally shaped wrong-target or wrong-actor bridge;
- keep an unaffected task invariant;
- charge repair information explicitly.

This creates the constructive counterpart to the humanistic loss.

## 8. R3-E — external humanistic transfer

After Yule–Cordier mechanisms are frozen, add at most one second historical editorial lineage chosen by source-structure criteria rather than expected effect.

Selection requirements:
- multiple scholarly/editorial layers;
- explicit backward pointers or revision apparatus;
- source objects openly reproducible;
- substantive historical interpretation task.

The second lineage is for transfer, not breadth-by-count.

## 9. Source-verification standard

Load-bearing historical relations require page-image verification when page images are available.

Gutenberg/OCR may:
- enumerate candidates;
- provide reproducible text search;
- support provisional offsets.

It may not alone establish a contested attribution/correction claim when the printed witness can be checked.

## 11. Manuscript consequence

The paper's central contribution is not:
"we compare several representations."

It is:
"we make the editorial history of scholarly claims itself auditable as a representation problem, and show how source-bound relations determine whether specific historical research tasks remain reconstructible."

Technical claims are subordinate to that humanistic object.
