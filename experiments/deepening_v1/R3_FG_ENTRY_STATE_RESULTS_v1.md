## Design

Four unique 1920 Addenda target entries were frozen before representation outcomes. The research entry state supplied an earlier 1903 locus and local seed, not the later target.

Routes:
- NATIVE_POINTER: reverse-follow chapter/page pointer in 1920 entry heads;
- BODY_LEXICAL: hide entry heads/pointers and rank 223 entry bodies using frozen seed-side TF-IDF;
- FULL_LEXICAL: rank head+body with the same rule.

- full lexical: rank 11 / 223.

All four unique targets are inside the top-20 under BODY_LEXICAL after the explicit later-entry head/pointer carrier is hidden.

## Supported result

The registered native pointer is not necessary for practical discoverability of these four targets under the frozen digital-object/TF-IDF contract.

At least one alternative textual carrier substitutes successfully in every selected target.

YC1920E-0021 gives a clear output-equivalent/profile-different case: the native pointer yields one direct candidate while the pointer-hidden textual route retains the target only at rank 8.

## Post-outcome diagnostic: carrier complementarity

YC1920E-0049 reveals a different pattern. The native page pointer alone yields two eligible later entries; the textual route ranks the correct target first.

This suggests a possible composition mechanism:

`pointer narrowing + content discrimination -> unique research path`

That mechanism was not a preregistered primary hypothesis for v1. It remains DEVELOPMENT_DIAGNOSTIC until frozen on untouched later entries.

## Negative lesson

More visible representation is not automatically better for a particular lexical policy. For YC1920E-0021, BODY_LEXICAL ranks the target 8 while FULL_LEXICAL ranks it 11.

This is not evidence that pointers are harmful. It shows that explicit pointer information and bag-of-words ranking are different operations; concatenating more head text can alter lexical ranking.

## Claim ceiling

The result supports carrier resilience and recovery-profile differences under this declared digital recovery contract.

It does not establish human effort, universal searchability, information-theoretic sufficiency, or a general superiority of any one representation.
