# R3 update-relation contrast amendment v4

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: frozen before any R3-F/G representation outcome.

## Problem

A later Addenda entry may contain an identification, attribution, or factual proposition that looks new when read in isolation but is already present in the earlier edition.

Therefore a 1920-only reading cannot establish an UPDATE or CORRECTION relation.

## Rule

The following labels require an explicit earlier-layer contrast:

- FACTUAL_CORRECTION
- IDENTIFICATION_UPDATE
- ATTRIBUTION_UPDATE
- TEXTUAL_UPDATE when "update" means change relative to the earlier edition rather than a standalone philological proposal.

A later act is not an UPDATE merely because it states an identification/attribution.

Required evidence:

1. later act and actor;
2. earlier target locus;
3. earlier target state;
4. contrast type:
   - CONTRADICTS_PRIOR;
   - REPLACES_PRIOR;
   - NARROWS_PRIOR;
   - ADDS_PREVIOUSLY_UNSTATED_ASSIGNMENT;
   - CORROBORATES_PRIOR;
   - NO_VERIFIED_CONTRAST.

Only the first four can support an UPDATE/CORRECTION label.

CORROBORATES_PRIOR must be coded as corroboration/additive evidence, not update.

## Immediate example

YC1920E-0030 (Pashai):

1920 Stein states that Polo had never personally visited the countries and that the account was derived from hearsay.

But the 1903 note already states there is nothing to suppose Polo personally visited the countries and that the information was probably derived from a Mongol source / hearsay geography.

Disposition:
- ATTRIBUTION_UPDATE: WITHDRAWN from the B02 first-pass interpretation;
- CORROBORATION / ADDITIVE_EVIDENCE: admissible;
- the historical importance remains high, but it is continuity rather than change.

## Consequence

R3-A has two distinct products:

1. INTERVENTION CONTENT:
   what the later entry says/does in 1920.

2. DIACHRONIC CHANGE:
   what differs from the earlier edition.

The second cannot be inferred from the first.

## Claim-strength effect

This rule prevents the inventory from inflating change by treating repeated or newly evidenced conclusions as revisions.

It also creates a substantive humanities result:
the Addenda may maintain, corroborate, contest, or change prior editorial knowledge, and these are empirically distinct.
