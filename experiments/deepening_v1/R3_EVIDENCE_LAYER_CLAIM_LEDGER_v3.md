# Updated capability decomposition

For a declared task, distinguish at least:

1. **Scope inclusion** — is the relevant material inside the declared representation horizon?
2. **Target discoverability** — can the relevant source unit be surfaced under the declared workflow?
3. **Carrier-set exposure/completeness** — does the surfaced representation expose every carrier required by the inquiry within the inspection budget?
4. **Relation determinacy** — once required carriers are supplied, do they uniquely determine the task relation under the contract?
5. **Responsibility/provenance traceability** — can responsibility and source basis be reconstructed?
6. **Resolution-state fidelity** — are uncertainty, qualification and contestation preserved?
7. **Composability/executability** — can represented relations be jointly used for the downstream scholarly operation?

No scalar "representation quality" score is licensed.

# Mother-problem answer after E3b

The strongest bounded answer is now:

> What must survive representational change is not a fixed maximal inventory of metadata. For a declared scholarly task and research entry state, a sufficient source-grounded carrier system must remain available or lawfully recoverable; the access representation must expose enough of that carrier system, within the declared inspection regime, for the task-relevant epistemic relations and statuses to be determined and composed. Content survival, target discovery, carrier completeness, relation determinacy and downstream executability are distinct conditions.

A useful index is:

[
Researchability(R,	au,S,K,pi;B)
]

where:

- (R) = representation;
- (	au) = scholarly task;
- (S) = research entry state;
- (K) = admissible source/carrier contract;
- (pi) = transformation/access workflow, including unitization/chunking and ranking;
- (B) = declared inspection budget when practical discoverability/exposure is evaluated.

(B) is not needed for pure information-theoretic determinacy; it is required for practical access/exposure claims.
