## Current scientific answer that is already supported

At the current bounded scope:

1. scholarly knowledge history is proposition- and responsibility-structured, not merely entry-structured;
2. preserving participating textual/propositional items does not determine target-specific stance, commitment/adoption, or evidence attribution when the corresponding bindings are removed in controlled projections;
3. source-grounded carriers can substitute or complement one another for discovery;
4. when a proposition-level binding is the missing controlled information, restoring the historically licensed binding recovers the inquiry while a matched wrong binding does not;
5. a natural plain-text representation can preserve those relations through alternative textual carriers;
6. preserving a recoverable relation does not guarantee that a frozen research workflow will surface the later treatment from a historical starting state;
7. researchability must therefore distinguish at least scope inclusion, discoverability, relation determinacy, provenance/responsibility traceability, uncertainty fidelity, and composability rather than treating representation quality as one scalar preserved/lost state.

## What is NOT yet answered

We do not yet know:
