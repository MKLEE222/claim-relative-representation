## What *is* observed

### 1. Entry-state relativity

The Great Desert contrast shows that researchability can change sharply with the starting locus even when source, target, representation class, and ranking policy remain fixed.

The broader chapter start produced substantially larger ranks; the exact historical note start made both later evidence carriers easy to surface.

Because the broader/exact contrast was diagnosed after the first successful run, it is a sensitivity result rather than a pre-registered causal estimate.

### 2. Native layer structure reduces access burden

The 1920 Addenda boundary is source-native, not target-engineered.

Restricting a later-treatment task to that layer consistently reduces candidate count and often improves ranks. Urumtsi provides the clearest single-carrier example: rank 58 -> 9.

### 3. Chunk completeness differs from collective recoverability

Pashai, Great Desert, and Tun-o-Kain do not place every registered carrier group in one 180-word chunk, yet a small collection of retrieved chunks can expose all carriers.

Therefore:

single-chunk sufficiency != workflow-level recoverability.

This is a representation/profile distinction, not evidence that the underlying scholarly relation has been deleted.

## Relation to the controlled R3 mechanism

The controlled proposition-binding separation remains valid:

The realistic workflow result now fixes the ecological claim ceiling:

No claim that plain text, chunking, or generic retrieval naturally deletes these bindings is supported.

## Consequence for the mother problem

The evidence now distinguishes three layers that must not be collapsed:

1. **source preservation** — the native plain-text witness retains the relevant historical carriers;
2. **representation determinacy** — controlled removal of proposition bindings can create same-visible-input/different-answer collisions;
3. **research access** — practical recoverability depends on entry state, native documentary horizon, retrieval policy, and whether multiple returned chunks may be composed.

This is a stronger and more bounded answer than a generic information-loss story.
