Date: 2026-09-27

## Finding

The earlier research-support decomposition remains directionally correct but was missing one empirically separable layer between target discovery and relation determinacy.

R3-AW1 Great Desert shows:

- the correct later entry can be rank 1;
- one required proposition carrier can also be rank 1;
- a second required carrier from the same entry can remain rank 35;
- no single 180-word returned unit contains both.

Therefore:

[
target discoverability

eq
task	ext{-}required carrier	ext{-}set completeness.
]

The capability decomposition must be expanded.

---

## C1 — scope inclusion

Question:

> Is the required source material inside the declared representation horizon at all?

Paper-money current-object condition provides a direct scope-exclusion example:
PM03 is outside the searchable object.

This is neither retrieval failure nor relation ambiguity.

---

## C2 — target/source-unit discoverability

Question:

> From the declared research entry state and workflow, can the relevant later source unit be surfaced?

R3-AW1 later-layer target-entry ranks:

- Great Desert 1 / 482;
- Arbre Sec 2 / 482;
- Pashai 4 / 482;
- Tun-o-Kain 13 / 482;
- Urumtsi 65 / 482.

Thus source material may be in scope while practical discovery burden differs sharply.

Urumtsi is the clean same-object example:
in scope and directly recoverable if supplied, yet outside B50 under the frozen lexical route.

---

## C3 — carrier-set exposure / completeness

Question:

> Once the correct source unit is discoverable, does the workflow expose all source-grounded carriers required for the full inquiry within the declared inspection budget?

R3-AW1 Great Desert provides the separation witness.

Under the native later layer:

- target entry rank = 1;
- folklore/source carrier rank = 1;
- measurement carrier rank = 35;
- one 180-word chunk contains both = false;
- both exposed within B20 = false;
- both exposed within B50 = true.

Under the full object:

- target entry rank = 6;
- folklore carrier rank = 6;
- measurement carrier rank = 341;
- both exposed within B50 = false.

Therefore:

[
Hit@K(entry) 
otRightarrow Complete@K(carrier set).
]

This is a workflow representation effect caused by unitization/chunking and ranking.
It does not imply deletion from the underlying source object.

---

## C4 — relation determinacy / recoverability

Question:

> Once the task-required carriers are supplied, does the visible representation uniquely determine the required historical relation under the declared contract?

Controlled R3 proposition-binding separations show that bags of actors/claims/stances/evidence can be identical while required answers differ when the binding relation is absent.

Examples:

- stance -> target;
- commitment/transmission/adoption;
- evidence -> proposition.

Scrivener and Whitman provide independent localization/relation-determinacy examples at other task levels.

Thus carrier exposure is not the same as relation determinacy.

---

## C5 — responsibility / provenance traceability

Question:

> Can the researcher recover who is responsible for an assertion or relation and which source locus licenses it?

R3 historical coding distinguishes cited scholar from transmitting editor.
Faust source-qualified relations distinguish assertion presence from source/status handling.

Textual co-presence alone does not license transfer of responsibility.

---

## C6 — resolution-state fidelity

Question:

> Does the representation preserve that a scholarly relation is uncertain, qualified, contested or unresolved rather than coercing it into a settled state?

Whitman certainty projection and R3 Pashai/Tun-o-Kain modality distinctions show that endpoint availability is not sufficient for uncertainty/status fidelity.

---

## C7 — composability / executability

Question:

> Can individually represented relations be jointly used for the downstream scholarly operation without an additional conflict-resolution policy or representation stage?

Digital Faust provides the strongest natural witness:

- raw/published base relation ecology contains conflict/cycles;
- downstream documented DAG is acyclic;
- simple ignore/delete filtering is insufficient to reproduce that stage.

Thus:

[
relation presence

eq
relation status

eq
executable representation.
]

# Updated research-support profile

For task (	au), representation (R), entry state (S), source/carrier contract (K), workflow (pi), and practical inspection budget (B), record a structured profile:

- **I** — scope inclusion;
- **D** — target/source-unit discoverability;
- **X** — carrier-set exposure/completeness;
- **Q** — relation determinacy/recoverability;
- **P** — responsibility/provenance traceability;
- **U** — uncertainty/resolution-state fidelity;
- **C** — composability/executability.

Do not collapse the profile into one scalar quality score.

For purely logical determinacy tests, (B) may be irrelevant.
For practical discovery/exposure claims, (B) is part of the evaluation contract.

A compact indexing is:

[
Researchability(R,	au,S,K,pi;B)
]

with (pi) explicitly including unitization/chunking, horizon restriction, ranking/navigation and lawful carrier mediation where applicable.

---

# Why C3 matters scientifically

Without C3, a retrieval evaluation can report success because the correct entry is found while still failing to expose the complete evidence basis needed by the scholarly question.

The Great Desert case demonstrates this directly:

[
entry=rank1,quad carrier_1=rank1,quad carrier_2=rank35.
]

A document-level Hit@K metric therefore under-measures a multi-proposition historical inquiry.

This is not a generic criticism of retrieval systems.
It is a task-relative measurement result derived from a frozen historical inquiry and an independently frozen access workflow.

---

# Claim ceiling

Supported:
- the seven capabilities are empirically separable in the current evidence portfolio;
- entry discovery and carrier-set completeness are distinct under the executed AW1 workflow;
- practical researchability should report the capability profile rather than one success bit.

Not supported:
- these seven dimensions form a universal ontology of scholarship;
- B50 is a universal human inspection limit;
- any one representation is globally superior;
- fixed-window retrieval necessarily causes historical error.
