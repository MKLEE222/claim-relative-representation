# Source-Grounded Mediation Contract v1

Date frozen: 2026-09-24
Status: PRE-EXPERIMENT CONTRACT

## Motivation

The current access results separate three phenomena:

- paper money: source/citation chaining plus native later-layer structure can sharply reduce retrieval burden;
- Kinsay: the generic lexical policy fails until a native referent-identity bridge is made available;
- external DH materials: relation, certainty, provenance, and task-facing structure are separable.

The next layer therefore treats source-grounded mediation as its own object rather than silently folding it into retrieval.

## Methodological ancestry

This contract borrows design discipline from the project's earlier work without importing its formal decision machinery.

Borrowed disciplines:

1. **support feasibility before complexity**
   - a mediation primitive must be instantiable from the frozen source/task state before its downstream retrieval effect is evaluated;
   - if the primitive cannot be formed, the result is N/A / support-unavailable, not "high mediation difficulty."

2. **fixed target**
   - a failed mediation primitive cannot be rescued by redefining the research target after the outcome is seen.

3. **source binding**
   - every bridge term or pointer must carry a source-facing basis and must bind to the declared referent/task.

4. **partial-state discipline**
   - changed, preserved, unresolved, and unavailable coordinates are reported separately.

5. **independent evaluation**
   - hidden target anchors are used only after candidate generation.

These are prior methodological assets, not claimed as new contributions of the DH paper.

## Mediation object

For task tau, representation R, and contract K, a mediation primitive m is admissible only when:

\[
Support(m\mid tau,R,K)=1.
\]

A supported primitive returns a typed bridge:

\[
m: S_{seed} \rightarrow B_m
\]

where every member of B_m is source-witnessed.

The bridge may then be composed with a retrieval/navigation policy pi:

\[
Retrieval(tau,R,K,pi,B_m).
\]

A retrieval improvement does not retroactively validate the bridge; bridge legality is decided first.

## Carrier ladder

A source-side distinction may occupy the following stages:

1. **HELD**
   - the distinction exists somewhere in the declared native object.

2. **ACCESSIBLE**
   - it lies inside the allowed evidence horizon.

3. **RECOVERABLE**
   - it can be extracted under the frozen recovery rule.

4. **COMPOSABLE**
   - it can legally enter the task's retrieval/relation workflow.

5. **WARRANT-AUTHORIZED**
   - a later humanistic adjudication licenses use of the distinction for a claim.

The current access experiments may establish stages 1-4.

Stage 5 remains sealed unless a separate claim-adjudication protocol authorizes it.

## Primitive family

### M_LOCAL_ATTACHMENT

Support condition:
- seed contains an explicit local note/pointer marker;
- a matching native note unit exists in the allowed local object.

Bridge:
seed passage -> attached note unit.

### M_REFERENT_ALIAS

Support condition:
- seed-side local context explicitly states that two or more names/forms identify the same declared referent.

Bridge:
surface form -> source-witnessed alias set.

No external gazetteer or target-only alias may be introduced.

### M_CITED_SOURCE

Support condition:
- seed-side text explicitly names a scholar/source relevant to the frozen task.

Bridge:
seed -> named-source search key.

### M_NATIVE_SEQUENCE

Support condition:
- task itself requests earlier/later treatment;
- native representation exposes documentary chronology/layer boundaries.

Bridge:
task direction -> eligible historical horizon.

### M_EXPLICIT_POINTER

Support condition:
- a native page/cross-reference/hyperlink points to another documentary locus.

Bridge:
source locus -> pointer target.

## Source-mediation obligations

### M1 - legality
Only information available from the frozen seed/task/native representation may instantiate the bridge.

### M2 - coverage
Every bridge token or target must have a recorded native basis.

### M3 - referent binding
The bridge must concern the frozen task referent rather than a same-string unrelated entity.

### M4 - operation fidelity
Normalization/extraction rules are frozen before target ranking is inspected.

### M5 - continuation fidelity
Composing the bridge with retrieval may change access burden but must not change the task target or success definition.

### M6 - verifier independence
Target-only anchors are absent from bridge construction and are used only for evaluation.

## Support stop

If a primitive fails its support condition:

\[
Support(m)=0
\]

the mediation experiment for that primitive stops.

The project must not:
- widen context after seeing failure;
- inject external aliases;
- switch to a target-derived name;
- change the task wording;
- reinterpret N/A as a negative effect size.

A different primitive or broader contract is a new experiment.

## Under-measurement versus legitimate task restriction

A cheaper or narrower representation cannot be called equivalent merely because the experiment also weakens the task.

Therefore:

- removing a carrier while holding tau fixed is a representation test;
- changing tau so that the missing carrier is no longer needed is a different scientific contract.

Both may be legitimate, but they are not the same result.

## Claim ceiling

This contract can support:

- source-grounded mediation availability;
- task-relative retrieval burden;
- carrier substitution/mediation;
- boundary/N/A results.

It does not by itself support:
- historical truth;
- universal representation rankings;
- final retain/revise/defer/withhold states.
